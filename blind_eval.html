Sports Video Evaluation

Instructions — Please Read Carefully

What is this about?

We are comparing two models that produce basketball video clips based on a text instruction. Each model is given a written description of what should happen in the video — for example, "Simulate Player in a red rectangle securing a rebound". The model then produces a video that tries to match this description.

Your job is to compare the two videos — labeled simply Model A and Model B — and tell us which one better follows the instruction and looks better.

What will you see?

Text Instruction — A written description of what the model was asked to produce. Players are referred to by colored rectangles — for example, "Player in a red rectangle". The color matches the rectangle drawn around that player in the videos. Read this carefully before watching the videos.

For each example, you will compare the two videos across 3 steps:

Step 1 — Target Player Positioning
You will see two videos (Model A and Model B) with colored rectangles drawn on them. The rectangle in the first frame shows where the target player starts, and the rectangle in the final frame shows where the target player should end up by the end of the video.

What to look for: Does the target player (identified by the colored rectangle in the instruction) move from the starting position to the correct final position? Is the player near the target rectangle at the end of the video?
A good video: the target player ends up at or near the specified target rectangle.
A bad video: the player is far from the target position, or the wrong player moves to that location.
Step 2 — Action Correctness
You will see the same two videos without the colored rectangles. Now focus on whether the action described in the instruction actually happens in the video.

What to look for:
- Is the correct player performing the action (check the colored rectangle from Step 1)?
- Does the action match the instruction? For example: if the instruction says "rebound", does the player grab the ball after a missed shot? If it says "foul", is there a foul-like contact? If it says "pick and roll", does the player set a screen and roll?
- Does the outcome match? For example, "missed shot" should show a miss, not a made basket.

You do not need basketball expertise — just check whether what you see in the video reasonably matches what the instruction describes. Use common sense.
Step 3 — Overall Quality
Considering everything — player positioning (Step 1), action correctness (Step 2), and general video quality — which video is better overall?

Think about: Does the video look sharp and realistic? Do players look like real people (not blurry or distorted)? Does the video flow smoothly? Combined with how well it follows the instruction — which one is better overall?

Tips

  • You do not need basketball expertise — just compare what you see against the written instruction.
  • Read the instruction carefully before watching the videos.
  • If both videos look equally good (or equally bad), you can select "About equal".
  • You can go back to change your answers using the "Previous" button.
  • Examples are randomly sampled and not cherry-picked.
Important: Please watch both videos carefully and fully before making your selection.

Please answer all questions before proceeding.
