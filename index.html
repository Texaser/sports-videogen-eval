Sports Video Generation Evaluation

Instructions — Please Read Carefully

What is this about?

We are evaluating AI models that generate sports videos (basketball and soccer). Each model receives a set of instructions describing where players should move on the field/court, and then generates a video trying to follow those instructions. Your job is to compare two models and tell us which one does a better job.

What will you see?

For each example, you will see a Ground Truth video and evaluate two AI-generated videos across 3 steps:

Ground Truth Video — This is the real, original video of the sports scene. The player trajectories are extracted from this video, and the generated videos are expected to follow these trajectories, but are not required to look exactly the same as the ground truth video. It is provided for scene context only.

Step 1 — Motion Trajectory Following
You will see two AI-generated videos (Model A and Model B), each with colored rectangles drawn on them. Each rectangle represents a target position — it shows where the AI was told to place a player at that moment.

What to look for: Do the generated players actually appear inside or close to these colored rectangles?
A good model: players closely follow the rectangles throughout the video.
A bad model: players drift away from the rectangles, appear in wrong positions, or are missing entirely.
Step 2 — Player Appearance
You will see the same two AI-generated videos, but now without the colored rectangles, so you can focus on how the players look.

What to look for: Do the generated players look like real people? Are their bodies, clothing, and movements realistic and consistent (e.g., no identity switching or distortion)? Or are they blurry, distorted, or flickering?
Step 3 — Overall Quality
Considering everything you observed in Step 1 and Step 2, which model produces the better video overall?

Think about: Does the video look sharp and clear? Does the scene (field, court, background) look realistic? Does the video flow smoothly without sudden jumps or glitches? Combined with how well players follow trajectories and how realistic they look — which model is better overall?

Tips

  • You do not need any sports knowledge — just compare what you see.
  • If both models look equally good (or equally bad), you can select "About equal".
  • There are 20 examples in total. You can go back to change your answers using the "Previous" button.
  • Examples are randomly sampled and not cherry-picked.
Important: Please watch all videos carefully and fully before making your selection. Submissions with insufficient viewing time may be rejected.

Please answer all questions before proceeding.
