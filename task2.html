Sports Video Generation Evaluation — Task 2

Instructions — Please Read Carefully

What is this about?

We are evaluating AI models that generate basketball videos based on a text instruction. Each model is given a written description of what should happen in the video — for example, "Player #10 performs a pick and roll" or "Defender #5 forces a missed shot". The model then tries to generate a video that matches this description.

Your job is to compare two models and tell us which one better follows the instruction.

How is this different from Task 1?

Task 1 asked: Do the players follow the movement paths? (trajectory following)
Task 2 asks: Does the video match the text instruction? (instruction following)

In Task 2, you will read an instruction describing what action should happen and which player should do it, then judge whether each model's video actually shows that action happening correctly.

What will you see?

For each example, you will see:

Ground Truth Video — The real video that the instruction was based on. It is provided for context only — the generated videos are not required to look exactly the same.

Text Instruction — A written description of what the AI was told to generate. Read this carefully before watching the generated videos.

Step 1 — Target Player Positioning
You will see two AI-generated videos (Model A and Model B) with colored rectangles drawn on them. These rectangles show where the target player should end up by the end of the video.

What to look for: Does the target player (identified by jersey number in the instruction) move to the correct position? Is the player near the target rectangle at the end of the video?
A good model: the target player ends up at or near the specified position.
A bad model: the player is far from the target position, or the wrong player moves there.
Step 2 — Action Correctness
You will see the same two generated videos without the colored rectangles. Now focus on whether the action described in the instruction actually happens in the video.

What to look for:
- Is the correct player performing the action (check the jersey number)?
- Does the action match the instruction? For example: if the instruction says "rebound", does the player grab the ball after a missed shot? If it says "foul", is there a foul-like contact? If it says "pick and roll", does the player set a screen and roll?
- Does the outcome match? For example, "missed shot" should show a miss, not a made basket.

You do not need basketball expertise — just check whether what you see in the video reasonably matches what the instruction describes. Use common sense.
Step 3 — Overall Quality
Considering everything — player positioning (Step 1), action correctness (Step 2), and general video quality — which model produces the better video overall?

Think about: Does the video look sharp and realistic? Do players look like real people (not blurry or distorted)? Does the video flow smoothly? Combined with how well the video follows the instruction — which model is better overall?

Tips

  • You do not need basketball expertise — just compare what you see against the written instruction.
  • Read the instruction carefully before watching the videos.
  • If both models look equally good (or equally bad), you can select "About equal".
  • You can go back to change your answers using the "Previous" button.
  • Examples are randomly sampled and not cherry-picked.
Important: Please watch all videos carefully and fully before making your selection. Submissions with insufficient viewing time may be rejected.
