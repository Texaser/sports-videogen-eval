Sports Video Evaluation — Task 1

Instructions — Please Read Carefully

What is this about?

We are comparing two models that produce short sports video clips (basketball and soccer). Each model receives a set of target player positions, and produces a video that follows those positions. For each example, you will see two videos side by side — labeled simply Model A and Model B — and we want to know which one looks better.

What will you see?

For each example, you will compare the two videos across 3 steps:

Step 1 — Motion Trajectory Following
You will see two videos (Model A and Model B), each with colored rectangles drawn on them. Each rectangle represents a target position — it shows where a player should be at that moment.

What to look for: Do the players actually appear inside or close to these colored rectangles? Also, players should only appear where there are rectangles — if a player appears in an area with no rectangle, that is a mistake. (Referees/officials are an exception and can be ignored.)
A good video: players closely follow the rectangles throughout the clip, and no extra players appear outside them.
A bad video: players drift away from the rectangles, appear in wrong positions, are missing entirely, or extra players appear where there are no rectangles.
Step 2 — Player Appearance
You will see the same two videos, but now without the colored rectangles, so you can focus on how the players look.

What to look for: Do the players look like real people? Are their bodies, clothing, and movements realistic and consistent (e.g., no identity switching or distortion)? Or are they blurry, distorted, or flickering?
Step 3 — Overall Quality
Considering everything you observed in Step 1 and Step 2, which video is better overall?

Think about: Does the video look sharp and clear? Does the scene (field, court, background) look realistic? Does the video flow smoothly without sudden jumps or glitches? Combined with how well players follow trajectories and how realistic they look — which one is better overall?

Tips

  • You do not need any sports knowledge — just compare what you see.
  • If both videos look equally good (or equally bad), you can select "About equal".
  • There are 30 examples in total. You can go back to change your answers using the "Previous" button.
  • Examples are randomly sampled and not cherry-picked.
Important: Please watch both videos carefully and fully before making your selection.

Please answer all questions before proceeding.
